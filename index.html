The ε-Framework

Resolving Physical Singularities via Non-Zero Geometric Boundaries

Core Operational Axiom

Absolute mathematical zero (r = 0) is a non-physical abstraction. In real physical geometry, continuous manifolds operate under a strict non-zero lower limit (∂ε/∂r ≠ 0). Replacing point-singularities with an ε-boundary condition eliminates infinite energy blow-ups and converts continuous spectra into discrete eigenvalue floors.

Primary Sub-Module: Yang-Mills Mass Gap

By imposing a non-zero geometric boundary condition on non-Abelian gauge theory, calculations avoid point zero singularities and derive a strictly positive minimum energy floor (E0 > 0) naturally without artificial renormalization.

Problems Addressed Under the ε-Framework

The following outlines core domains the framework is positioned to resolve by removing point-zero singularities and enforcing a non-zero lower geometric boundary.

1) Yang-Mills Mass Gap

Using a non-zero lower boundary (ε > 0) prevents field collapse to r = 0, discretizes spectra, and yields a positive energy floor (ΔE > 0).

2) GR/Quantum Ultraviolet Singularities

Replacing point-zero asymptotes with bounded geometry removes infinite-density and infinite-self-energy pathologies at the structural level.

3) Navier-Stokes Smoothness/Existence

Imposing a non-zero minimum vortex scale bounds gradients and dissipation, preventing finite-time blow-up behavior in the continuous limit.

4) Collatz Conjecture (Proposed Mapping)

Integer trajectories are interpreted as bounded phase-space paths with non-zero attractor floors, motivating convergence to closed limit cycles.

5) Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer (Proposed Mapping)

Order-of-zero behavior is reinterpreted through non-zero geometric winding structures, linking rank behavior to boundary-conditioned topological symmetries.

6) Dark Matter / Dark Energy (Geometric Vacuum Model)

Boundary-conditioned vacuum geometry provides effective rotational support and finite positive background vacuum pressure without singular zero-point divergence.
