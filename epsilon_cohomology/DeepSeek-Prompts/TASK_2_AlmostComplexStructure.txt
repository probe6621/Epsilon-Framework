## FORMAL GOAL

Prove that the almost complex structure J_ε on T(X × T² × iT²) satisfies J_ε² = -id.

## MATHEMATICAL BACKGROUND

An almost complex structure on a smooth manifold M is a (1,1) tensor field
J : TM → TM (an endomorphism of the tangent bundle) such that:
  - J² = -id (composition J ∘ J equals negative identity)
  - J is smooth (depends smoothly on the base point)

On the product manifold X_ε = X × T² × iT², we construct J_ε to act:
  - On T_x X (tangent to X): inherit any existing structure or act trivially
  - On T_{[0]} T² ≅ ℝ: the standard complex structure (rotate by 90°): v ↦ -v (after doubling)
  - On T_{[0]} iT² ≅ ℝ: similar action
  
The key property is that J² = -id, which is equivalent to:
  ∀ v ∈ TM, J(J(v)) = -v

## LEAN THEOREM SIGNATURE TO FILL

theorem epsilonAlmostComplex_squares_to_neg_id
    (x : M × (ℝ × ℝ) × (ℝ × ℝ)) :
    let J := epsilonAlmostComplexStructure M
    ∀ v : TangentSpaceAt (M × (ℝ × ℝ) × (ℝ × ℝ)) x,
      (J.J x) ((J.J x) v) = -v := by
  sorry

## HIGH-PRECISION CHAIN-OF-THOUGHT DECOMPOSITION

**Step 1: Understand the Almost Complex Structure Definition**
- An AlmostComplexStructure is a structure containing:
  * J : ∀ x, TangentSpaceAt M x →L[ℝ] TangentSpaceAt M x (continuous linear map)
  * J_smooth : proof that J depends smoothly on x
  * J_squares_to_neg_id : proof that (J.J x) ∘ (J.J x) = -id
- We are asked to prove the last property at the algebra level

**Step 2: Understand the Product Tangent Space**
- If x = (x₀, (t₁, t₂), (s₁, s₂)), then:
  TangentSpace ℝ (M × ℝ² × ℝ²) x ≅ TangentSpace ℝ M x₀ × ℝ² × ℝ²
- A tangent vector v = (v_M, (v_t1, v_t2), (v_s1, v_s2))

**Step 3: Define J Component-Wise**
- J acts on the product as: J(v_M, (v_t1, v_t2), (v_s1, v_s2))
     = (J_M(v_M), J_T(v_t1, v_t2), J_iT(v_s1, v_s2))
- where J_M, J_T, J_iT are component-wise structures

**Step 4: Compute J² for Each Component**
- On T_x M: J_M² = -id (assumed or inherited from existing structure)
- On T_{[0]} T² ≅ ℝ: The standard complex J acts as multiplication by i, so:
    J_T(v₁, v₂) = (-v₂, v₁)  [rotation by 90°]
    J_T²(v₁, v₂) = J_T(-v₂, v₁) = (-v₁, -v₂) = -(v₁, v₂) ✓
- On T_{[0]} iT² ≅ ℝ: Similarly, J_iT² = -id ✓

**Step 5: Combine Component Results**
- On the product:
  J²(v_M, (v_t1, v_t2), (v_s1, v_s2)) 
    = (J_M²(v_M), J_T²(v_t1, v_t2), J_iT²(v_s1, v_s2))
    = (-v_M, -(v_t1, v_t2), -(v_s1, v_s2))
    = -(v_M, (v_t1, v_t2), (v_s1, v_s2))
- Therefore J² = -id on the entire product

**Step 6: Formalize in Lean**
- Use ContinuousLinearMap.comp to express J ∘ J
- Use neg_smul or scalar multiplication to express -id
- Apply component-wise reasoning via Pi.prod_mk_apply or similar

## TACTIC PROOF SKELETON

```lean
theorem epsilonAlmostComplex_squares_to_neg_id
    (x : M × (ℝ × ℝ) × (ℝ × ℝ)) :
    let J := epsilonAlmostComplexStructure M
    ∀ v : TangentSpaceAt (M × (ℝ × ℝ) × (ℝ × ℝ)) x,
      (J.J x) ((J.J x) v) = -v := by
  intro J v
  -- Unfold J to the product structure
  unfold epsilonAlmostComplexStructure at J
  -- Decompose v into components
  simp only [Prod.mk.eta] at v
  -- Apply J twice on each component
  sorry  -- component-wise computation
```

Or using a more direct approach:
```lean
  intro J v
  -- Each component satisfies J² = -id
  have hM := sorry    -- from J_M being almost complex on M
  have hT := sorry    -- from rotation on T²: (-v₂, v₁) after (-v₁, -v₂) is -(v₁, v₂)
  have hiT := sorry   -- from rotation on iT²
  -- Combine using product
  simp [hM, hT, hiT]
  ring
```

## KEY MATHLIB LEMMAS TO USE

- `ContinuousLinearMap.comp`: Composition of continuous linear maps
- `ContinuousLinearMap.comp_eq_neg_id`: Pattern for J ∘ J = -id
